What Is UPI AutoPay?

UPI AutoPay is used for eligible recurring payments through a mandate. A mandate records permission for future debits under the terms shown when it is created. It is an ongoing payment instruction, not simply a one-time payment; review its scope before approving it.

Before you create a mandate

  • Start from a merchant, biller or subscription that you recognize and intend to pay.
  • Check the merchant name, fixed or maximum amount, frequency, start and end dates, validity and any other conditions displayed.
  • Find out where the mandate can be viewed, paused or revoked, and whether you must separately cancel the merchant's subscription or service.

Steps to review and approve AutoPay

  1. Choose the recurring service on the merchant's official website or app and follow its supported UPI mandate flow.
  2. Open the mandate request in the UPI app you trust. Do not approve a request that appears unexpectedly.
  3. Compare the merchant, purpose, amount or maximum amount, frequency and validity with the service you selected.
  4. Authorize the mandate only if you understand and accept the future debit permission shown on screen.
  5. Return to the app's mandate section and confirm the instruction's status. Keep the confirmation for your records.

What to check afterwards

Review active mandates and upcoming debit details periodically. Read notifications from your bank or app and compare a debit with the mandate and merchant bill. When ending a service, check both the mandate status and the merchant's subscription status; revoking a payment mandate may not cancel the separate service contract.

The Reserve Bank of India's 2026 e-mandate framework sets requirements for recurring transactions, including pre-transaction alerts and transaction limits, with specified exceptions and categories. Check the current framework and the exact conditions shown by your issuer; do not assume every mandate has identical rules.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Approving a mandate without checking the merchant, maximum amount, frequency and validity.
  • Assuming that pausing or revoking a mandate automatically ends the underlying subscription or bill.
  • Assuming every bank and app exposes the same mandate-management options.
  • Ignoring a pre-debit notification or a debit that does not match the mandate terms.

What Is a UPI Mandate?

A UPI mandate records permission for a future or recurring payment under stated conditions. Before approving one, check the payee, maximum or fixed amount, schedule, validity period and the options available for viewing or revoking it.

How to Create an AutoPay Mandate

Create a mandate only from a service you recognize and intend to pay. Read the amount or maximum amount, frequency, start and end dates, merchant identity and cancellation path on the confirmation screen before approving.

How to Approve a Recurring Payment Request

A recurring-payment approval creates or authorizes a payment arrangement, so treat it as a continuing permission rather than a one-time checkout. Confirm that the request matches the subscription or bill you chose and reject unfamiliar requests.

How to View Your Active Mandates

The app or bank that manages a mandate usually provides a view of relevant active, pending or revoked instructions, though menu names differ. Check the current status and details there rather than relying on an old confirmation message.

How to Check the Amount and Date of an AutoPay Payment

Compare the scheduled debit date and amount shown for a mandate with the bill or subscription notice. Amounts can be fixed or limited by a maximum, so review the mandate terms and the merchant’s invoice together.

How to Pause an AutoPay Mandate

Pausing is available only when the relevant provider and mandate support that action. Confirm the status after pausing and check whether the merchant still expects a payment through another method; pausing a mandate may not pause the service itself.

How to Cancel or Revoke an AutoPay Mandate

Revocation generally concerns future debits under that mandate, but it may not terminate the separate contract or subscription with the merchant. Complete any merchant cancellation required and verify both the mandate status and the service status.

What Happens If an AutoPay Payment Fails?

A failed recurring debit can have several causes, including insufficient funds, a mandate condition or a provider issue. Check the transaction and mandate status, read the merchant’s retry or late-payment terms, and avoid assuming that the mandate has been cancelled.

How to Manage Subscription Payments

Subscription management often involves two separate controls: the merchant account and the UPI mandate used to collect payment. Review both places when changing or ending a subscription so you understand whether access, future billing or both have been stopped.

What Happens When a Mandate Is Debited?

When a mandate is used, compare the posted debit with the mandate terms and the merchant’s billing record. If the amount, date or merchant does not match, preserve the transaction and mandate references and raise the issue with the bank or merchant through official support.

How to Manage Mandates After Changing UPI Apps

Changing a UPI app does not guarantee that every mandate is transferred or managed in the new app. Check the bank’s mandate record and the original merchant or provider flow, then confirm where each active instruction can be viewed, changed or revoked.

Practical checks before you act

An AutoPay mandate is an authorization for future debits; review the mandate scope and schedule before approving it.

  • Check merchant name, maximum amount, frequency and validity.
  • Review active mandates periodically in the app or bank channel.
  • Use the official mandate-management flow to pause or revoke when supported.

RBI's Digital Payments – E-mandate Framework, 2026 applies to recurring transactions using cards, PPIs and UPI. It requires a mandate validity period and a facility to modify or withdraw the mandate; issuers generally send a pre-transaction notice at least 24 hours before a debit. Subsequent recurring transactions may be processed without AFA up to ₹15,000 per transaction, with specified categories such as insurance premiums, mutual-fund subscriptions and credit-card bill payments having a ₹1,00,000 threshold. Check the current issuer/app rules and the exact applicability to your mandate. Keep confirmation of any change or cancellation.
